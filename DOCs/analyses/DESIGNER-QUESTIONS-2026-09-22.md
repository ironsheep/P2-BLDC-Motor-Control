# The board designer's questions — hall phase, back-EMF, and whether each motor is unique

**Asked:** 2026-09-22, relayed by Stephen. **Answered from:** the Visit 7c pass 2 evaluation
(`bench/2026-09-22/VISIT-7C-PASS2-EVALUATION.md` §3.3, §3.5, §3.6) and the motor manual
(`../MOTOR-6.5IN-TECHNICAL-MANUAL.md` §2, §4, §9.1). Both answers cover the **two** 6.5″ units on the
Rev B platform. The two Rev A units have not been measured.

---

## Q1

> *"I know that the hall effect sensors give some indication of position, which has some relationship
> to electrical position and mechanical position. I'm totally not understanding how to read the back
> EMF as any indication of position. It's possible that on each motor, there is a static phase
> difference between the hall effect sensors and the electrical poles. And that difference may be
> unique."*

### What the halls tell you

The three halls give one of six codes. Each code is a 60° **electrical** sector. This motor has 30
magnets (15 pole pairs), so the six-code pattern repeats 15 times per wheel revolution. That makes 90
hall ticks per revolution, 4° mechanical per tick. We confirmed this by turning a wheel three times by
hand and counting 270 transitions (manual §2.2).

So the halls tell you **electrical** position directly, but only to the nearest sector. They tell you
**mechanical** position only by counting ticks from a starting point. Electrical position is the one
that commutation needs.

### How back-EMF indicates position

When the wheel turns, each magnet sweeps past each stator winding and induces a voltage in it. With
the bridge switched off (coasting), each of the three phase terminals therefore carries a sine wave
**generated by the motor itself**. Nothing in the drive shapes that wave; its shape comes from the
magnets passing the windings.

The key property: **each phase's zero crossings, and each phase's peak, happen at fixed rotor angles
— fixed to where the magnets are relative to that winding.** They do not depend on speed (only the
amplitude and the time between crossings do), and they do not depend on current, because there is
none. So a back-EMF crossing is a mark on the rotor's true electrical angle.

Record the hall edges and the back-EMF crossings on the same clock, and you can see where each hall
edge falls against the magnets. **That offset is exactly the static phase difference you describe.**

In practice (pass 2 §3.3):

1. The wheel is turned by hand with the bridge coasting. The P2 samples the three phase voltages and
   timestamps both the hall edges and the phase-voltage crossings.
2. Each crossing is placed as a fraction of the way through its hall sector. That gives its position
   in the **hall frame**.
3. The three phases' peaks must fall 120° apart, in the order the drive's phase convention predicts
   (U → W → V for forward). They did: 64.2°, 184.4°, 305.1°. That checks the frame rather than
   assuming it.
4. We use the **peak** of each phase (the midpoint between its rising and falling crossings) rather
   than the crossings themselves. Our sense channels clip the negative half-wave, which shifts both
   crossings but leaves their midpoint unmoved.
5. The difference between the hall frame and the magnet frame is the hall zero, **Z**.

### Is there a static phase difference? Yes — and it is small, and we have it

| Motor (Rev B) | Z from back-EMF, coasting | Z from a driven current-minimum scan |
|---|---:|---:|
| LEFT | **−3.31°** electrical | −3.8° (eighth speed) · −3.85° (quarter) |
| RIGHT | **−3.25°** electrical | −4.0° (eighth speed) · −3.45° (quarter) |

- **It is static.** Slow and brisk hand-turns agree within 0.12°, and the driven scan shows Z moving
  under 0.6° across a doubling of speed. A property of sensor placement should behave this way.
- **Two methods agree.** The coasting method uses no drive, no current and no offset sweep, and the
  driven method uses all three. They agree within about 0.35–0.7°.
- **The library already carries it.** The shipped offset corresponds to Z = −4°, correct to within
  0.7°, so no change is needed.
- In mechanical terms, −3.3° electrical is about **0.22° mechanical**. That is a tight placement
  tolerance on the sensors.

## Q2

> *"Are you finding that each motor is unique in its electrical phase versus hall effect phase?"*

**No, not in any way that matters to the drive — for the two units we have measured.**

- **The zero offset is not unique.** LEFT −3.31° and RIGHT −3.25° differ by 0.06° electrical,
  well inside the measurement's own scatter. One constant serves both.
- **What IS unit-specific is small: the six sectors are not exactly 60° each.** On the LEFT motor
  sector `101` is always about 61° and `001` about 59°, with the same pattern on every leg, both
  directions, both speeds and both runs. That is the sensors' physical placement. LEFT spreads ±0.9°
  around 60°; RIGHT ±0.55°. A drive that commutates in 60° sectors cannot see ±1°. It would matter
  only if we interpolated inside a sector, where it would set about a 1° error floor.
- **Hall lag is ±1° each way, and it is an angle, not a time delay.** Forward reads about 2° more
  negative than reverse, at both 43 and 117 edges/s. A time delay would grow with speed; this does
  not. It behaves like hall-sensor hysteresis, and averaging the two directions cancels it.

**The limit on this answer:** two units are not a population. Four units of this motor exist. The
Rev A pair can be measured the same way: the method needs no drive and no current, so Rev A's
coarser current sense does not affect it. First, though, we need to confirm that the Rev A board
presents phase voltage at the same scale as Rev B (manual §9.1).

## What this does and does not say about back-EMF for driving

Everything above was measured **coasting**. There, back-EMF is clean from 43 hall edges/s upward
(2–5° scatter per crossing, and the lower limit has not been reached). Whether it stays readable
**while the bridge is driving**, and below 43 edges/s, has not been measured. That question decides
whether back-EMF can become a position input to the drive, and it belongs to the drive-integration
design (`../plans/DRIVE-INTEGRATION-DESIGN.md` §1.3).
