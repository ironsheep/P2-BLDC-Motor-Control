# The 6.0.0 sprint — retrospective, 2026-10-01

**Sprint:** Bench Readiness (2026-09-10) and its sub-plan, Plan A, the window-free bench (2026-09-30). **Build:** v6.0.0,
tag `ccef603`, published 2026-10-01. **Closeouts:** `2026-10-01-BENCH-READINESS-CLOSEOUT.md`,
`2026-10-01-WINDOW-FREE-BENCH-CLOSEOUT.md`.

## Discovered perspectives

- A patch sprint became a driver rework: the audit behind a user's faulting-wheel report found missing pieces, not bugs.
  The plan absorbed it through 18 dated Sprint Revisions rather than a re-plan, and it stayed readable only because each
  revision said which text it superseded.
- The least-current commutation placement is the one nearest the torque peak, so the timing that wins wheels up leaves
  the trim loop the least margin under a real platform's inertia (PL-167). Wheels-up certification cannot see it.
- The 8-bit lag error wraps at ±128. A hold at 86 plus one backward hall sector (42.7) trips the 125 fault test; at 100 it
  wraps safely. The hold threshold is constrained by the error's width, not only by the torque curve.
- Hands-off floor sessions (a fenced 2 m square, chained checks) produced more certified loaded behaviour in one visit than
  four attended attempts did: the countdown-board runs produced no driver reading at all.
- Desk models earned their keep (the start surge, the servo relay, D-4's wrap), and so did their limits: the refit showed
  the phase-1 "proven at the quarter speed" depended on fitted parameters.
